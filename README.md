# Lynxo — Archived Project
[![DOI](https://zenodo.org/badge/1337090656.svg)](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21979071)
**Status: paused by the author's own decision, not abandoned.** Everything
needed to understand, verify, run, or continue this work is in this
archive. See `Lynxo_Kadero_Project_Report_v1.md` for the full account —
philosophy, complete mathematical foundation with proofs, and the full,
honestly-reported experimental log across five phases of testing on IEEE
14/30/118 and a 300-bus system.

## What this is, in one paragraph

Lynxo proposes the **Kadero Embedding**: a way of representing a power
network as a point in a high-dimensional vector space built from
effective-resistance/spectral graph theory, geography, topological
redundancy, and (optionally) the AC operating point — each channel backed
by a proof, each parameter left free for later calibration against data.
The mathematics is complete and, for its two most important claims
(a conservation law and an exact bridge-detection theorem), independently
verified against ground truth. The empirical question — does this add
real predictive value over classical methods — is currently an honest
**null result**: on the networks tested, the framework has not yet shown a
statistically clear advantage over a simple classical baseline, though it
has been shown, repeatedly, to capture something genuinely different from
classical edge betweenness centrality. Full detail, including two
self-caught and openly corrected errors, is in the main report.

## For anyone continuing this work

The roadmap in Section 6 of the paper is a prioritized, honest list of
what to test next and in what order, with a pre-registered bar for what
would count as a real result rather than noise. Section 4.7 flags one
experiment (`severity_variants_case300`) as containing a likely bug,
documented rather than hidden — worth fixing before trusting its
conclusion either way.
